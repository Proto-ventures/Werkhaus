# Positioning

Internal. This is the document every other piece of copy has to agree with.

## The category problem

"AI builds your company" is a crowded, discredited category. Search it and you
get a dozen tools that take an idea and return a confident business plan with an
invented TAM, three competitors (one of which doesn't exist), and an ICP nobody
checked. The output is fluent, unfalsifiable, and wrong in ways a non-technical
founder cannot detect.

That produces two kinds of buyer, and neither converts well:

- **The credulous one** signs up, gets the plan, takes it to an investor meeting,
  finds out the numbers are made up, and blames the tool. High churn, bad word of
  mouth, no referrals.
- **The burned one** has already tried two of these. They are not skeptical of AI;
  they are skeptical of *confident* AI. Ordinary marketing bounces off them.

Competing on "better output" is a losing move here, because the buyer cannot
evaluate output quality — that is the whole problem. Everyone's demo looks good.

## The wedge

**Werkhaus is the one that shows its working and tells you what it made up.**

This is not a copy angle. It is enforced in the product:

| Claim | What actually backs it |
|---|---|
| Every document is labelled `sourced`, `inferred`, or `assumption` | A schema validator. A document cannot be saved as "sourced" without the URLs an employee actually opened. |
| One employee is paid to attack the work | Vera, the critic. Runs at the end of every shift, always, even when the budget is gone. Files at least three falsifiable objections naming a specific claim and what evidence would settle it. She has no write access — she can flag, not fix. |
| You watch it happen | Live shift board. Eight named employees, their current activity, the documents appearing as they are written. |
| One output is unfakeable | The landing page either builds and loads, or it doesn't. Everything else is prose; this is the ground truth that carries credibility for the rest. |
| You can stop it | A hard spending cap and a stop button that takes effect in under two seconds. Partial work is kept. |

The strategic bet: **in a category where nobody can judge the output, the buyer
buys the thing that helps them judge the output.**

## Who this is for

A non-technical founder — the kind who would otherwise pay an agency £4,000 for a
market scan and a landing page, or spend three weekends doing it badly themselves.
They can tell a good idea from a bad one. They cannot tell a researched number
from an invented one, and they know it.

They will never see a terminal, a repository, a commit, or an error message. The
abstraction is a company with employees doing shifts.

## Who this is not for

- Anyone who wants the AI to *run* the business. It does groundwork, not judgment.
- Technical founders who would rather drive the tools themselves. They are better
  served by the coding agents underneath this.
- Anyone shopping on price against a $20/month plan-generator. Our floor cost per
  shift is real money and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

## The one sentence

> Werkhaus gives you a small team of AI employees who research your idea, price
> it, position it, and put a real page on the internet — and it marks every claim
> so you know which parts to trust.

## What we will not claim

Written down because the temptation is constant and the whole position dies the
first time we overclaim.

- Not "validated". Ever. Competitor pricing is *defensible*, not *validated*.
  Only a customer validates anything.
- Not "launch your startup in an hour."
- Not "replaces a co-founder / an agency / a research team."
- Not "finds you customers." It drafts outreach. It never sends anything.
- No made-up market-size numbers in our own marketing, for obvious reasons.

If a claim would embarrass us in front of a customer who read the shift report
carefully, it does not go on the site.
