# Early client acquisition, before there is a product

The honest constraint: right now Werkhaus can run a convincing shift on a stub.
It cannot yet run a real one on an arbitrary idea — that lands in M4. Any outreach
that promises a real report before then will burn the one asset we have, which is
being the trustworthy one in a category of overclaimers.

So the motion runs in two stages, and **stage 1 does not start until a real shift
works end to end.**

---

## Stage 0 — now. Ask for time, not money.

**Goal:** twenty conversations. Find out whether "it marks its own guesses" is a
hook or a shrug. That is the only thing worth learning right now, and no amount of
landing-page polish answers it.

**The ask:** fifteen minutes, and permission to show a three-minute demo.

**What you're testing, in order:**

1. Do they recognise the problem unprompted? Listen for "I didn't know what to
   believe" or "I asked ChatGPT and it just agreed with me." If you have to
   explain the problem, the wedge is weaker than we think.
2. When Vera's objections appear, do they lean in or glaze over? This is the
   whole bet. Watch their face, not their words.
3. What would they have paid the agency instead? Gets you a price anchor without
   asking about price.
4. What would they *not* trust us with? The boundary of the product.

**Do not** pitch. Do not ask them to sign up for anything. There is nothing to
sign up for, and asking makes the next conversation harder.

### Cold email — stage 0

Subject: `the AI business-plan thing, but honest`

> Hi {name},
>
> I'm building something in the "AI writes your business plan" space, which I'm
> aware is a category full of nonsense.
>
> The bit I think everyone gets wrong: the output always sounds certain, so you
> can't tell which numbers came from a real page and which the model invented.
> Ours marks every claim as sourced, inferred, or made up, and there's one agent
> whose entire job is to attack the other seven's work in front of you.
>
> I'm not selling anything — there's nothing to sell yet. I'm trying to find out
> if that's actually the thing that bothers people or if it's just what bothers
> me. Fifteen minutes some time this week?
>
> {your name}

Why it works: it concedes the category's bad reputation in line one, which is the
only way past a burned buyer. It has no call to action beyond time. It admits
there is no product, which is disarming and also true.

### The DM version

> Saw your post about {specific thing}. I'm building an AI thing that does the
> market-research-and-landing-page grind, and the whole point is that it labels
> which bits it made up rather than sounding confident about everything. Trying to
> work out if that matters to anyone but me — worth 15 min?

### Community post

For indie founder forums, no-code communities, build-in-public threads. Give
first; do not link the site in the body.

> **I built the AI business-plan tool that admits when it's guessing**
>
> Every one of these tools I tried produced a market size, three competitors and a
> target customer, all in the same confident voice, and I had no way to tell which
> parts were real. One of the competitors it gave me didn't exist.
>
> So the version I'm building labels every document: sourced (it opened these
> URLs), inferred (it reasoned from something sourced), or assumption (it made it
> up to keep going). And there's a critic agent that has to file at least three
> specific objections against the other agents' work every run, with what evidence
> would settle each one. It can't fix anything, only flag.
>
> Here's a real objection from a test run: {paste a Vera objection}
>
> Genuinely unsure whether this is the thing people want or whether everyone just
> wants the confident version. Happy to run it on your idea if you want to poke
> holes.

### Where to go

Ordered by signal, not volume:

1. **Founder communities where people post their idea for feedback** — the ask
   ("can I run this and send you the folder?") is native to the venue.
2. **No-code communities** (Bubble, Softr, Webflow, Makerpad forums). Squarely our
   audience: they build, they cannot code, they are used to paying for tools.
3. **Accelerator and co-founder-matching alumni lists.** Pre-seed founders doing
   exactly this groundwork by hand right now.
4. **Consultants and fractional CMOs.** Underrated. They do this work for clients
   repeatedly and will tell you in five minutes what's missing. Some become a
   channel.
5. **Local founder Slacks and meetups.** Low volume, very high signal, and you get
   to watch faces.

Deliberately not: paid ads, Product Hunt, cold LinkedIn at scale. Nothing to
convert them to yet, and a launch you can only do once should not be spent on a
stub.

## Stage 1 — after a real shift works. Give the artifact away.

**The offer:** send us your idea, we'll run a company on it and send you the
folder. Free. In exchange, you tell us what you didn't believe.

This works because the deliverable is genuinely valuable on its own, arrives
before any commitment, and produces the two things we need most: real feedback,
and a shareable artifact with the founder's own business in it.

### The delivery email

Subject: `your Werkhaus shift report — {company name}`

> Hi {name},
>
> Your shift is done. Here's the folder: {link}
>
> Start with Vera's objections rather than the research. She found {n} things,
> and the one worth reading first is {paste the strongest objection verbatim}.
>
> Two things to know:
>
> - The landing page is live at {url}. It works — open it on your phone. The
>   waitlist stores a real address.
> - The money model is entirely assumptions. It says so on every line. Don't take
>   it to anyone until you've had one conversation with a supplier.
>
> The blunt question: what in here did you not believe? I'd rather hear the
> uncomfortable version.
>
> {your name}

Leading with the criticism rather than the research is the whole brand in one
gesture. It is also just true — the objections are the most useful page.

### Turning it into a loop

Every delivered report should end with a share link the founder can post. That is
the only viral mechanic worth building pre-revenue: a founder showing people what
their company looks like, with our name on it, and with the objections visible.
A share page that hides the criticism is worth nothing to us.

## What to measure

Small numbers, honestly counted. Vanity metrics are the same failure mode as
invented market sizes.

| Stage | The number that matters | Not this |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Conversations where they described the trust problem **before you did** | Emails sent |
| 0 | People who leaned in at Vera | Demo requests |
| 1 | Of the reports delivered, how many founders replied with a real criticism | Reports delivered |
| 1 | Founders who shared their link unprompted | Page views |
| 1 | Founders who asked "can I run another one?" | Waitlist size |

The last one is the only genuine demand signal before there is a price. Someone
asking for a second shift has told you more than a thousand signups.

## Pricing, when it comes

Not on the site yet, but the shape is decided by the cost floor, so it is worth
writing down.

A full shift costs roughly $4 typical, $10 worst case in model spend. Any
unmetered subscription under about $1,000/month is insolvent. So: **credits, not
seats.** The user buys shifts. The dashboard already speaks in "shifts left"
rather than dollars, which is the unit a founder thinks in.

Do not launch a $29/month unlimited plan. It is the one pricing decision that
cannot be undone.
